= Defining O5 — the veridicality oracle (a proposed §10 entry)
:revnumber: 0.1 (proposal — for owner ratification into the calculus)
:revdate: 2026-07-17
:toc: macro

[abstract]
The estate cites *calculus O5* as the obligation behind "veridicality is declared and
propagated, not checked" (trope-checker `README.adoc`, `AFFIRMATION.adoc`, the co1
operational-model note §5) — but `spec/calculus.adoc §10` stops at O4: *O5 has never
been written down*. This note closes WP-2 of the research programme by *proposing* the
missing entry: a two-part obligation consisting of an impossibility remark (veridicality
is not decidable from the IR alone) and an oracle *interface* with named soundness
conditions for deployments that want more than declared veridicality. Per estate
doctrine (invariants belong to the formal layer, owner-ratified), this is a *proposal*:
the normative home is `trope-checker/spec/calculus.adoc §10`, and nothing here is
binding until adopted there.

toc::[]

== The gap, precisely

Three firewalls guard the checker's honesty. The third says: _veridicality is declared
and propagated, not checked_ — detecting that a value claimed faithful is in fact false
needs a ground-truth oracle the checker does not have. Every statement of this firewall
cites "calculus O5". But §10 of the calculus defines only O1–O4. The citation chain
therefore ends in the air: an obligation that cannot be discharged, refuted, or even
read. The ratification wave (R-2026-07-07 / ADR 0004) did not touch this — verified
against trope-checker HEAD, §10 still stops at O4.

== Why the checker cannot do it (the impossibility half)

The point is almost definitional, which is precisely why it deserves to be a named
remark rather than folklore:

[IMPORTANT]
====
*Remark O5-a (IR-relative undecidability of veridicality, proposed).* A valid Trope IR
is a set of *claims*: grades asserted by a front-end about what happened to
property-instances. For any IR document `D` and any field of any node, there exist two
worlds — one in which the claimed value is the value the trope in fact had, one in
which it is not — that induce the *same* `D`. Since the checker is a pure function of
`D` (HC-1: the IR is the trust boundary), no checker can decide veridicality. What the
calculus *does* guarantee (and machine-checks) is strictly weaker and strictly
load-bearing: deceptive grades are two-sided zeros on the ratified carrier — they do
not compose away — and they fail every honest floor.
====

This turns Firewall 3 from an apology into a theorem-shaped boundary: the checker's
silence about truth is not a missing feature but a consequence of what an IR *is*.

== What a deployment may supply instead (the interface half)

Where more than declared veridicality is needed, the obligation moves *outside* the
checker, to the system that has access to the world. Proposed normative text, in the
calculus's own idiom:

[NOTE]
====
*O5 (the veridicality oracle, proposed).* An implementation MAY bind a *veridicality
oracle*: a judgement external to the IR,

    Oracle ⊨ record r faithful-to trope t  (per field φ ∈ Φ)

subject to three conditions:

* *O5-1 (soundness).* If the oracle affirms field φ of `r`, then the honest grade
  claimed for φ is true of the world; the oracle MUST NOT affirm a `Falsified`,
  `Misbound`, or `Conflated` situation.
* *O5-2 (attestation).* Every oracle verdict MUST be carried as evidence — who checked,
  against what access to the world, when — and that attestation is itself a record
  (it enters the `record` field's provenance, not the quality's value).
* *O5-3 (no laundering).* An oracle verdict upgrades *confidence*, never *grade*: a
  checked `Attenuated(δ)` is still `Attenuated(δ)`. The oracle answers "was the claim
  honest?", not "was nothing lost?".

Falsifier: exhibit a deployment where an interface satisfying O5-1..3 nevertheless lets
a deceptive grade reach a `p-sufficient` verdict against an honest floor. Upgrade path:
mechanize O5-1 as a side condition on an `Oracle`-indexed variant of the verdict
judgement.
====

== Where the estate already has the shape for this

The interface is not invented from nothing — two estate components already have the
right slots, which is evidence the definition carves at a real joint:

* *Vocarium's warrants.* The store's objects include *warrants* attached to
  transformation paths. A warrant is exactly an O5-2 attestation: named evidence that a
  claim about a particular was checked against something outside the record. O5 gives
  warrants their formal job description.
* *Hermeneia's `licensed` band.* The query layer separates *retrieved / interpretable /
  licensed*. "Licensed" is the verdict band that O5-backed claims occupy: retrieved
  says the record exists, interpretable says it composes, licensed says someone with
  world-access vouched (O5-1) and left an attestation (O5-2).

== Status and next step

This note is the WP-2 deliverable in its *define* branch (the *refute* branch — that no
useful oracle interface exists — is answered by the constructions above existing).
Adoption path: the owner ratifies a §10-O5 entry in `trope-checker/spec/calculus.adoc`
(this text or an amended version), updates the README/AFFIRMATION citations to point at
it, and — when Vocarium's warrant schema lands — names O5-1..3 as the conditions its
warrants discharge. Until then, every "calculus O5" citation should be read as pointing
at this proposal.
