= Grades as abstractions of transformations: one reproduced counterexample and three open theorems
:revnumber: 0.1 (research note — open questions, nothing ratified)
:revdate: 2026-11-17
:toc: macro
:sectnums:
:stem: latexmath

[abstract]
The grade calculus is an algebra of _loss shapes_. This note records a point made
in a design review of the trope stack on 2026-11-06 and not yet written down in any
repository: a grade abstracts a transformation, and the relation between the two is
not yet a theorem. It gives one counterexample, reproduced on the shipped checker,
in which the same grade is sufficient for a use under one transformation and
insufficient under another; and it states three theorems the programme would need
in order to say that a grade verdict means what its name says. Nothing here changes
the calculus. Its normative home is `trope-checker` `spec/calculus.adoc`, and any
adoption is the owner's.

== The counterexample

=== On paper

Let the source be stem:[D = \mathrm{Bool} \times \mathrm{Bool}], with states
stem:[(a, b)]. Take two transformations and one use:

[stem]
++++
f_1(a,b) = a, \qquad f_2(a,b) = b, \qquad u(a,b) = a.
++++

Each transformation keeps exactly one Boolean predicate of the source. With the
predicate's name erased, each is described by the same fate, `Predicated`.

stem:[f_1] is sufficient for stem:[u]: the answer stem:[u] needs is the value
stem:[f_1] kept. stem:[f_2] is not: the source states stem:[(0,0)] and stem:[(1,0)]
give the same observation stem:[0] under stem:[f_2] and need different answers.

So no verdict that reads only the erased grade can be both sound and complete for
every use. It may refuse both. It cannot accept one and refuse the other.

=== On the shipped checker

The calculus writes the fate as `Predicated(P)` and keeps the predicate
(`spec/calculus.adoc`, _Coordinate 1 — field fate_). The verified developments erase
it, and say so (same file, _Composition_: "The verified developments (§9.3) erase
predicate and fusion labels"). The shipped
Rust checker compares the erased fate.

Two IR documents were made from the conformance fixture
`tests/conformance/fixtures/duck-collapse.ir.json`. Both set the use-model floor on
`fate.quality` to `Predicated` with predicate `isQuack`. They differ only in the
predicate the `collapse` edge records as having survived.

[source,sh]
----
for p in isQuack isLoud; do
  jq --arg p "$p" '
      .edges[0].grade.fate.quality.predicate = $p
    | .use_model.floor.fate.quality = {k: "Predicated", predicate: "isQuack"}' \
    tests/conformance/fixtures/duck-collapse.ir.json > edge-$p.json
  src/rust/target/release/tropecheck-rs edge-$p.json
done
----

[source]
----
edge predicate isQuack, floor predicate isQuack -> p-sufficient
edge predicate isLoud,  floor predicate isQuack -> p-sufficient
----

The second verdict is the counterexample. The use asks for `isQuack`; what survived
is `isLoud`; the checker reports `p-sufficient`.

=== What this is and is not

It is *not* a breach of any proved law. L1–L6 and the soundness results are stated
over the erased carrier and assert nothing about label equality; the calculus says
that in so many words.

It *is* a gap between the verdict's name and its content. The calculus's first
firewall (_Soundness, and the firewalls_) reads: "Soundness, not completeness. The checker MAY be conservative:
it MAY report `p-insufficient` where the true residual would in fact have sufficed."
That allows a false refusal. The case above is a false _acceptance_, which the
firewall does not cover.

Whether the remedy is to compare predicate labels in the floor check, to refuse a
`Predicated` floor outright, or to move the question into a finer semantic layer
(§3) is a normative decision and is not made here.

== The proposed reading

The review's proposal, recorded as a proposal:

[quote]
A trope is an instance-level subject of interpretation. Its identity is not its
representation. Transformations act on representations and carry evidence about
what they preserve. Grades abstract those transformations. Use-models specify the
distinctions required of the result. A sound verdict depends on the relationship
between all four.

Three consequences for the vocabulary:

. *Particularity is not what is graded.* What is graded is how recoverable
information about a particular is from a representation of it.
. *The grade algebra stays, as a coarse abstraction.* The answer to the
counterexample is a finer, use-sensitive layer above it, in preference to a tenth
effect or a fourth coordinate.
. *A failed check is not a proved failure.* The verdict has three possible
meanings, of which the IR names two:
+
[cols="1,2"]
|===
| Outcome | Evidence it needs

| Certified sufficient
| A sound proof that the distinctions the use needs survive.

| Certified insufficient
| A concrete counterexample, or a proof that a needed distinction cannot survive.

| Indeterminate
| The abstraction or the evidence cannot establish either.
|===
+
Two readings of `p-insufficient` are in use, and they differ. This workbench's
vocabulary (`README.adoc`, the verdict table) defines it by what it is taken to
mean: "The surviving residual is _not enough_ for the declared use. Something
needed was lost (or falsified)". What the checker computes is narrower, an
operational result: the declared grade failed the declared floor. The second
implies the first only if the grade is exact. Where the grade is a conservative
estimate, the residual may in fact have sufficed, which is the false refusal the
calculus allows. So the operational result is evidence for the vocabulary's
reading and not a proof of it.
+
Trope IR v0.2's `p-insufficient` can keep its operational meaning without a schema
change. Under the proposed reading, the vocabulary's sentence would describe
_certified insufficient_, and a bare failed floor test would be _indeterminate_
unless a counterexample comes with it. Whether to reword the vocabulary is the
owner's decision and is not made here. This bears on Hermeneia's _licensed_ band.

== Three open theorems

=== An abstraction theorem

Let stem:[\gamma(g)] be the set of concrete transformations a grade stem:[g]
conservatively describes. Define

[stem]
++++
\mathrm{ProducerSound}(f, g) \iff f \in \gamma(g), \qquad
\mathrm{AbstractSafe}(g, u) \iff \forall h \in \gamma(g).\ \mathrm{Suff}(h, u).
++++

Then

[stem]
++++
\mathrm{ProducerSound}(f, g) \wedge \mathrm{AbstractSafe}(g, u) \implies \mathrm{Suff}(f, u).
++++

The implication is immediate. The work is in defining a useful stem:[\gamma], in
proving that producers satisfy it (this is O2, lowering correctness), and in
computing stem:[\mathrm{AbstractSafe}] soundly. The counterexample of §2 says that
for the erased `Predicated` grade and the use stem:[u], stem:[\mathrm{AbstractSafe}]
is false.

=== Sufficiency as factorisation

stem:[\mathrm{Suff}(f, u)] should mean that stem:[u] factors through stem:[f]:
there is a stem:[v] with stem:[u = v \circ f]. Equivalently, any two sources that
stem:[f] cannot tell apart, stem:[u] does not need to tell apart. The open items
are the statement over the calculus's typed transformations, and its behaviour
under composition.

=== Coherence-preserving atom factorisation

The atom-factorisation theorem (`Trope.GradeR.atom_factorization`) factors a grade
into its slot projections in the product carrier. The calculus also imposes
coherence between slots: an absent bearer with an `Intact` bond is malformed. A
coherent `project` drops the bearer and marks the bond `Withheld` in one step.
Factoring that grade slot by slot can pass through an intermediate grade with an
absent bearer and an intact bond.

The open question: can every well-typed transformation be decomposed into
well-typed, coherence-preserving primitive transformations, and not only into
coordinate factors of an abstract product? If not, the present theorem stands as
mathematics and is not a completeness theorem for the executable calculus. This
bears on O1 and should be read beside WP-5.

== What would falsify the proposed reading

* A semantic layer that decides §2 correctly only by becoming as large as the
transformations it describes. The abstraction would then be buying nothing.
* A stem:[\gamma] under which every interesting grade is unsafe for every
interesting use. The grade algebra would then be sound and useless.
* A coherence-preserving factorisation that needs primitives outside the nine
effects. That would reopen the effect typology (ADR 0002).

== Relation to the programme

This note proposes no new work package. It gives WP-5 (O1 and coordinate adequacy)
a concrete test case and gives O2 a statement of what a producer proof has to
establish. The review's suggested order of work puts the concrete semantics and
the abstraction theorem before further carrier theorems.
