= Where Vocarium could serve: a survey with four experiments and one proposed theorem
:revnumber: 0.1 (survey — nothing ratified, nothing adopted)
:revdate: 2026-11-17
:toc: macro
:sectnums:
:stem: latexmath

[abstract]
Vocarium is the store of the trope stack. This note asks whether it has a use in
four other parts of the estate: the Palimpsest licence's logic, the chora identity
registry, VeriSimDB's consonance subjects, and the cadastre. Each question is put to
the built store engine and the shipped checker, in that order, with small fixtures
kept in `examples/survey/`. It also compares the two things the estate calls a
_warrant_ and states, without proving, a theorem that would join them. It changes no
specification, licence, proof or sibling repository.

== Experiment 1: the Palimpsest licence's logic

=== What Palimpsest asks

Read from `palimpsest-license` (`legal/PALIMPSEST-MPL-1.0.txt` §2 to §4,
`spec/PROVENANCE-SPEC.adoc`). The framework's own terms:

* _Emotional Lineage_: "the narrative, cultural, symbolic, and contextual meaning
embedded in" a work.
* _Provenance Metadata_: signed attribution information.
* A _Non-Interpretive System_ "processes Covered Software without preserving or
considering its Emotional Lineage".

Its rules are about what a transformation keeps. A non-interpretive use must be
documented and must "not claim that outputs … carry the Emotional Lineage". Nobody
may "strip, alter, or obscure Provenance Metadata".

This is the shape of a trope and a grade. The work's context slot holds the lineage,
its record slot holds the provenance metadata, and a transformation's grade says what
became of each.

=== The run

`examples/survey/e1-palimpsest.jsonl` holds one work and two non-interpretive
ingestions of it. Both drop the context. One keeps the provenance metadata and one
strips it. Two use-models ask for different things.

[cols="2,2,3"]
|===
| Output | Use-model | Verdict

| metadata kept | `carries-emotional-lineage` | `p-insufficient`, witness `e_ni_kept`, `fate.context`
| metadata kept | `provenance-intact` | `p-sufficient`
| metadata stripped | `carries-emotional-lineage` | `p-insufficient`, witness `e_ni_stripped`, `fate.context`
| metadata stripped | `provenance-intact` | `p-insufficient`, witness `e_ni_stripped`, `fate.record`
|===

The first row is Palimpsest's rule that such an output must not be claimed to carry
the lineage, as a refusal that names the edge and the slot. The fourth is the
stripping rule. The second is the permitted case.

=== Consent has a scope, and the checker does not read it

`examples/survey/e1c-consent-scope.jsonl` models a consent grant. One reading of it
keeps `Predicated(consented-for-research)`, another `Predicated(consented-for-training)`.
The use-model's floor asks for `Predicated(consented-for-training)`.

[cols="2,3"]
|===
| Reading | Verdict under `needs-training-consent`

| consent for training | `p-sufficient`
| consent for research only | `p-sufficient`
|===

The second line is wrong. It is the false acceptance recorded as trope-checker
issue 125, in the setting where it matters most: consent for one use satisfying a
floor that asks for another. Palimpsest's own model refuses this by a different
route (§6): its rule 4 holds a consent claim at the middle level whatever evidence
is offered.

*Until issue 125 is settled, the trope stack must not be used to decide consent.*

=== What this experiment does not show

* It does not show that the grades in the fixture are true of any real pipeline. A
grade here is a claim with a warrant id beside it (§6).
* Palimpsest's sources disagree with each other about training: its README says
"Permitted with Attribution", `config.ncl` defaults to `prohibited`, and
`PALIMPSEST.adoc` says "explicit consent". Which is intended is the licence author's
decision. This note takes no side and changes nothing there.
* Emotional lineage has structure in Palimpsest's own metadata (context, trauma
markers, cultural origin, ancestors, descendants). One context slot flattens that.

== Experiment 2: the history of a chora

=== What the registry wants

Read from `standards` `2-protocols/chora/CHORA-SPEC.adoc` (draft, not ratified) and
`chora-registry`. A chora is the identity record of a repository. Its id "is minted
once, from its birth path", and "a validator never re-derives it from the current
owner and name, so a transfer, rename or move does not change it" (§9). The
registry's planned schemas want an append-only status history, lineage events
(rename, merge, split), observations with evidence, attestations, and withdrawn ids.
Its database is not deployed. For one kind of record the estate reconciliation
documents already accept a plain directory in its place: "The envelope store is a
directory the farm keeps until a store exists. VeriSimDB replaces it behind the same
interface" (A4, F15).

=== The run

`examples/survey/e2-chora-history.jsonl` records a real rename: the registry itself
was `gv-clade-index` and is now `chora-registry`. The trope is the identity, and its
bearer is the path. The rename keeps the id and drops the path.

[cols="2,3"]
|===
| Use-model | Verdict

| `resolve-by-id` | `p-sufficient`
| `follow-old-path` | `p-insufficient`, witness `e_rename`, `fate.bearer`
|===

The specification's rule about ids and paths comes out as a pair of verdicts: a
reference by id survives a rename, and a reference by path does not, and the refusal
names the rename.

=== The gaps

. *Withdrawal.* The registry has `(retired …)` records. Vocarium refuses a
`revocation` record: "revocation records are reserved for M2". Run.
. *Time.* The registry's history is ordered. Vocarium's log is append-only and
ordered by line, and a record carries no time of its own.
. *Signed attestations.* Vocarium's warrant is an id and an optional text (§6).

=== The boundary

A chora _is_ identity. Vocarium stores particulars and what became of them. The
fixture stores an observation about an identity and a transformation of how that
identity is reached. Putting the identity record itself in Vocarium would cross the
line CHORA-SPEC draws between the chora and everything derived from it, and the line
Vocarium's own charter draws around the checker's algebra. The specification lists
"the trope format" as out of scope for now, which leaves the question open and not
answered.

== Experiment 3: a peer of a consonance subject

=== What VeriSimDB offers

Read from `verisimdb` (`README.adoc`, `docs/modality-profile.adoc`). An entity is a
_consonance subject_. Its _modality set_ is the modalities it has, a subset of the
_modality profile_ an implementation supports. This implementation's profile is the
_Octad_: eight fixed slots (graph, vector, tensor, semantic, document, temporal,
provenance, spatial). The eight are still fixed; what changed is that a subject need
not fill them all.

An outside store joins as a federated peer through an Elixir behaviour with five
callbacks: `connect`, `query`, `health_check`, `supported_modalities`,
`translate_results`. A peer declares the modalities it can serve.

=== What Vocarium could declare

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Modality | What Vocarium holds for it

| provenance | edges: what was transformed into what, by which effect, on whose warrant
| semantic | tropes: quality, bearer, context, record
| graph | the transformation paths between tropes
| temporal, vector, tensor, document, spatial | nothing
|===

By Vocarium's ADR-0002 (wire, do not link) the adapter would be Elixir code in
`verisimdb` that runs `voc`, and nothing of VeriSimDB's would enter Vocarium.

=== The measured gap

The only thing `voc` emits today is the IR for one `invoke`. Its edges have the keys
`effect`, `grade`, `id`, `inputs`, `output`, and its nodes `id`, `present`, `type`.
The warrant id and the four descriptive slots are in the store and are not in the
output. Run. A provenance peer needs exactly those. Hermeneia refuses `show warrant`
for the same reason. So the first piece of work is a read path in `voc` that returns
stored records, and it serves Hermeneia's M2 adapter as much as VeriSimDB.

=== What each side has that the other lacks

VeriSimDB measures drift between two modalities as a number in stem:[[0,1]], and its
query language can ask `DRIFT(m1, m2) < 0.1`. That score is symmetric and has no
purpose in it. Experiments 1, 2 and 4 each show one transformation that is sufficient
for one use and insufficient for another. No single number says both.

VeriSimDB has time. Experiment 4's cadastre row is four months old, and nothing in
the grade calculus can say so.

A grade as a drift metric, and a temporal modality beside a trope, are both
proposals. Neither is designed here.

== Experiment 4: a cadastre row

=== What the cadastre is today

Read from `metadatastician/cadastra`. It scans the two organisations and writes
`docs/status/estate-triage-report.csv`: one row per repository with a classification
(CLEAN, MIGRATE, KILL, DONE, FORK) and anti-pattern flags. Its documents call it the
observer and say it is not the identity registry. It has no front end. Its own
arrival pack says "maturity experimental; 5% complete". The "verify mode" that the chora
documents rely on is not built.

=== The run

`examples/survey/e4-cadastre-row.jsonl` takes row 299 as written: vocarium, primary
language Nickel, classification CLEAN, last activity 2026-07-08. The scan reduces a
repository to a label, so the edge keeps `Predicated(classification)`.

[cols="2,3"]
|===
| Use-model | Verdict

| `triage-queue` | `p-sufficient`
| `describe-the-repository` | `p-insufficient`, witness `e_scan`, `fate.quality`
|===

The label is enough to queue the repository and not enough to describe it. The row
bears that out: it says Nickel, and vocarium is a Zig store engine.

=== What a front end would gain

A front end over rows stored this way could answer "is this row enough for what I am
about to do with it" and name the scan as the reason when it is not. That is a
proposal with nothing to run: there is no front end, and the scan is a shell script
that writes a CSV. The nearer step is the cadastre's own verify mode.

== Two things called a warrant, and a theorem that would join them

=== The two definitions

*epistemic-types* (`src/EpistemicTypes/Warrant.agda`, at `bfcee6e`) gives three
records over a standpoint stem:[\kappa] and a claim stem:[A]:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| `Warrant κ A` | a type of evidence for stem:[A]
| `Epi κ A` | a warrant and one token of its evidence
| `SoundWarrant κ A` | a warrant and a map from its evidence to stem:[A]
|===

Palimpsest (`proofs/agda/Provenance/Warrant.agda`, `formats/warrant.ncl`) uses these
by pin as its three levels `warrant`, `epi` and `sound`. It proves six statements
over them in Agda, rechecked in Rocq and Lean with no axioms. Three matter here:

* `no-signature-sound`: without the unforgeability assumption, no uniform map takes
a verified signature to "signed by".
* `no-consent-sound`: no uniform map takes a licence header to consent. A consent
claim is at most `epi`.
* `no-transfer`: a sound warrant for one claim, held with a token for another, does
not give the other.

*Vocarium* (`src/store/store.zig`) has a warrant record `{id, text?}`. Every edge
must cite one that exists, or the store refuses the edge. The store reads nothing
else of it.

=== The comparison

They are one notion at two levels of detail, and Vocarium's is the lowest.

. A Vocarium edge is a claim: _this transformation has this grade_. Its warrant
record names that there is something the claim rests on. In epistemic-types' terms
that is a `Warrant` with its evidence type left unsaid and no token held: level
`warrant`. With the text filled in it is at most a token: level `epi`.
. Nothing in Vocarium can express `sound`. There is no place to name a rule or an
assumption.
. The checker's verdict is the other half. Given the grades, it _computes_ whether
they compose above the floor, and that computation is what the stack's proofs are
about. That is a sound rule, for the claim "these declared grades are enough for
this floor".
. So `p-sufficient` today joins a sound step to a step held at `warrant` or `epi`.
By the reading of `no-transfer`, the result is not sound for the claim people take
it to make: that the transformation itself suffices for the use.

This is the gap _Grades as abstractions of transformations_ names from the other
side, where it is the difference between stem:[\mathrm{AbstractSafe}(g,u)] and
stem:[\mathrm{Suff}(f,u)]. The warrant levels give it a type.

=== The proposed theorem

Write stem:[P(f,g)] for "transformation stem:[f] is described by grade stem:[g]"
(producer soundness, O2), stem:[S(g,u)] for "grade stem:[g] meets the floor of use
stem:[u]" (what the checker decides), and stem:[\mathrm{Suff}(f,u)] for "stem:[u]
factors through stem:[f]".

*W-1, the level of a verdict.* For every standpoint stem:[\kappa]:

[stem]
++++
\mathrm{SoundWarrant}\ \kappa\ P(f,g) \;\to\; \mathrm{SoundWarrant}\ \kappa\ S(g,u)
\;\to\; \mathrm{SoundWarrant}\ \kappa\ \mathrm{Suff}(f,u)
++++

given the abstraction lemma stem:[P(f,g) \to S(g,u) \to \mathrm{Suff}(f,u)]. The
evidence of the result is the pair of the two evidences.

*W-2, no upgrade.* There is no uniform map

[stem]
++++
\mathrm{Epi}\ \kappa\ P(f,g) \;\to\; \mathrm{SoundWarrant}\ \kappa\ S(g,u) \;\to\; \mathrm{Suff}(f,u).
++++

A verdict computed over an edge held only at `epi` is itself at most `epi`.

*W-3, along a path.* For a path of edges, the level of the verdict is the least
level among them.

What each would take:

* W-2 should follow from `no-transfer` by choosing the claims. It is the cheap one
and the one with consequences: it says what `p-sufficient` may be called.
* W-1 needs the abstraction lemma, which is the open theorem of the earlier note. It
is as hard as that.
* W-3 needs an order on the three levels. epistemic-types has the three records and
`sound-epi`; an order with a meet was not found there in this reading.

If W-2 holds, three things follow, each a decision and not a consequence:

. Vocarium's warrant record would need a level, and for `sound` a rule and its
assumption. Palimpsest's contract already has those fields.
. A verdict would carry a level. Hermeneia's _licensed_ band is the place it shows.
. The consent case of §2 gets a second, independent refusal: whatever the checker
says about the predicate, a consent edge is at most `epi`, so the verdict is too.

None of this is proved, and proving it belongs to the proof work and its kernels.

== For Mathesis

Mathesis keeps an atlas of the estate's mathematics and derives status from the home
repositories' receipts. Read at `69b7f0f`:

* Its census frame already names every repository this note touches: trope-checker,
vocarium, hermeneia, haec, palimpsest-license, epistemic-types, verisimdb, cadastra.
* It keeps bridge candidates, relations between artefacts in two repositories, in
`docs/atlas/claims/bridges.jsonl`. It has two.

This note offers three candidates. They are proposals for that register and were not
written to it.

[cols="2,3,2"]
|===
| Candidate | Endpoints | State to record

| A Vocarium warrant is an epistemic-types `Warrant` held at most at `epi`
| vocarium `src/store/store.zig` (no proof); epistemic-types `Warrant.agda` (Agda)
| unassessed; read, not checked

| The verdict's level: W-2 as an instance of Palimpsest's `no-transfer`
| palimpsest-license `Provenance/Warrant.agda` (Agda, rechecked); trope-checker verdict soundness (Agda, Idris2)
| unassessed; statement only

| Sufficiency under a use is the purposeful, directed counterpart of VeriSimDB's drift score
| verisimdb `formal/Drift.v` (Rocq); trope-checker `spec/calculus.adoc`
| unassessed; no formal statement on the trope side
|===

Two cautions for the atlas. A receipt in a home repository is itself a claim with a
warrant, and Mathesis's reader derives a grade from it; the levels of §6 and the
Proof Witness Grades are answering one question in two vocabularies, and nobody has
said how they relate. And the fixtures here are not proofs and earn no grade.

== What would decide the next step

In order of what each unblocks:

. *Issue 125.* Until predicate labels are compared or refused, experiment 1c stays a
false acceptance and consent stays out of scope.
. *W-2.* It is small, it rests on a theorem already proved three times, and it
settles what a verdict may be called.
. *A read path in `voc`.* One piece of work serves Hermeneia's adapter, a VeriSimDB
peer and any front end.
. *Revocation in Vocarium.* Needed before it can hold any history that includes a
withdrawal.

What would count against the whole direction:

* If W-2 is false as stated, the two warrant notions are namesakes and §6 falls.
* If every useful Palimpsest question turns out to need the predicate's content, the
grade algebra adds nothing there beyond naming the slot that was lost.
* If the chora specification, once ratified, places lineage inside the identity
record, experiment 2's separation does not hold.
