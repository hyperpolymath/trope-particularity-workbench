= Trope Theory and Neural Representation — a position paper
:revnumber: 0.2 (draft for review; synced to ratified calculus R-2026-07-07)
:revdate: 2026-07-16
:toc: macro
:sectnums:
:sectnumlevels: 3

[abstract]
A neural network learns *features*. The unexamined question is what kind of thing a
learned feature _is_. The default answer — a feature is membership in a shared,
repeatable category, a universal — is a metaphysical commitment, not a fact about the
network. This paper proposes the alternative: treat a learned feature as a *trope*, an
abstract particular — a property-instance, situated and non-repeatable — and read the
network's known pathologies (superposition, polysemanticity, the binding problem) as
what the estate's vocabulary already calls *loss and deception* of property-instances.
Everything here is offered as *research directions and hypotheses*, not results; it is
explicitly not a claim that trope theory is a training method.

toc::[]

== The unspoken metaphysics of a "feature"

Interpretability research speaks freely of the features a model has learned — a
"masculine pronoun" feature, a "Golden Gate Bridge" feature — and treats them as
*universals*: one repeatable property, shared across every input that exemplifies it.
Trope theory (Williams 1953; Campbell 1990; Maurin, _SEP_) offers a different ontology:
properties are *abstract particulars*. There is no shared "rednesses"; there is _this_
redness, here, and _that_ redness, there, resembling but numerically distinct.

The wager of this paper is that the trope ontology is the better fit for what a network
actually contains. A network does not store the universal "edge"; it stores a great
many *edge-instances*, each a particular activation in a particular context, and our
interpretability tools then _abstract_ a universal out of them — an act of abstraction
that, in trope theory, is exactly the move that must be handled with care. The estate's
existing distinction between *exact resemblance* and *numerical identity* — the
distinction `p-conflating` violates — is precisely the distinction an interpretability
method blurs when it declares two activations "the same feature."

== The four classic problems, as machine-learning questions

The four canonical problems of trope theory turn out to be four standing questions of
representation learning. (This table is the shared spine of the estate's programme of
work; see `docs/status/RESEARCH-PROGRAMME.adoc`.)

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Classic trope problem | The metaphysical question | The neural-net translation (proposed)

| *Individuation*
| What makes _this_ property-instance the one it is?
| What counts as *one* distinct learned feature? When is an activation a feature-instance versus noise? (Per-instance vs per-class features.)

| *Numerical identity vs exact resemblance*
| Two tropes exactly alike are still two.
| When are two activations "*the same feature*" versus merely resembling? Deduplicating or merging features without erasing their distinctness.

| *Swapping*
| Permuting two indistinguishable tropes changes nothing.
| Permutation-(in)variance of exactly-alike feature-instances; when is a representation right to be order-blind, and when does order carry identity?

| *Piling / stacking*
| Many exactly-alike tropes co-located on one bearer.
| *Superposition* and *polysemanticity*: many features packed into one unit; honest aggregation versus a silent collapse that reasons about one where there were several.

| *Resemblance-regress*
| Grounding similarity without positing a universal "resemblance".
| Metric learning: how a network grounds "similar" without a dedicated universal feature for similarity — and whether provenance can stand in for it.

| *(Bearer / compresence)*
| Whose property is it? An object as a bundle of compresent tropes.
| The *binding problem*: attributing features to the right object, keeping multiple objects' features from interfering.
|===

== The estate's loss/deception effects are names for known failure modes

The workbench's nine effects (`vocabulary/`) were derived to describe what happens to a
property-instance as it passes through _any_ system. Read against a neural network, they
name failure modes the interpretability literature already studies — but names them
_diagnostically_, separating honest loss from deception, and both from mere noise.

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Effect (workbench) | What it names in general | The neural-net reading (hypothesis)

| `p-collapsing`
| A quality flattened to a predicate/scalar — "the quack becomes `true`".
| A rich feature-instance reduced to a single logit or a binary probe result: the individuating content is gone, the test remains.

| `p-attenuating`
| Fidelity degraded by an unknown, structureless amount.
| Lossy compression in a bottleneck layer with no clean account of _what_ was lost — the honest "I lost some, amount unknown".

| `p-detaching`
| A quality severed from its bearer.
| A feature that fires free of the object it belongs to — a binding failure of the "which object?" kind.

| `p-fusing` (honest)
| Two instances blended into one *labelled* aggregate; plurality recoverable.
| Superposition done *recoverably*: multiple features share a unit but a sparse dictionary (an SAE) can pull them apart again — the label survives.

| `p-conflating` (deceptive)
| Two distinct instances asserted to be one, *unlabelled*.
| *Polysemantic interference*: two features collapsed with no signal that there were two, so downstream reasons about one and is silently wrong.

| `p-falsifying` / `p-misbinding` (deceptive)
| A false value / a false bearer, asserted as faithful.
| A probe or explanation that attributes a feature the model did not have, or binds it to the wrong token/object, while presenting as faithful.
|===

The pay-off of the vocabulary is the *veridicality axis*: the honest twin `p-fusing`
(recoverable superposition) is categorically distinct from the deceptive `p-conflating`
(silent, lossy collapse). Interpretability today largely lacks a principled name for
that distinction — the difference between a network that _compresses recoverably_ and
one that _confuses irrecoverably_. The estate has one, and a checker that treats the
deceptive move as a validation-boundary fault.

== Four research directions (falsifiable, provisional)

. *Instance-level features.* Build (or probe) a representation that carries
  property-instances with the workbench's four coordinates — quality, bearer, context,
  and an honest `record` — rather than class memberships. Hypothesis: provenance-carrying
  features are more robust to the binding failures catalogued by Greff et al.

. *Recoverable vs irrecoverable merging.* Instrument superposition with the
  `p-fusing` / `p-conflating` distinction. Hypothesis: features an SAE can cleanly
  separate behave as honest `Fused τ`; features that resist separation and produce
  interference behave as `Conflated`, and the checker's grade predicts where downstream
  errors concentrate.

. *Use-relative sufficiency for probes.* Adopt the verdict discipline: a feature that is
  `p-sufficient` for one task (counting) can be `p-insufficient` for another
  (diagnosis). Hypothesis: a declared use-model + the loss grade predicts probe transfer
  failure better than accuracy alone.

. *Swapping and piling as invariance tests.* Use the (currently open) formal images of
  swapping and piling (programme WP-1) to design controlled probes of permutation
  behaviour and superposition capacity. The calculus's composition `▷` is *commutative*
  on the ratified carrier (R-2026-07-07 A1) — the natural formal image of
  swapping-*invariance* ("permuting indistinguishables changes nothing") — so the probe
  is whether that commutativity matches empirical order-invariance, with any residual
  order-*sensitivity* attributed to routing/DAG structure rather than to `▷`.

== What this is *not*

* *Not a claim that trope theory is a neural-net algorithm.* It is an ontology and a
  diagnostic vocabulary. It reframes and names; it does not train.
* *Not a claim of results.* Every hypothesis above is unfalsified _and untested_. Per
  the estate publication gate (`docs/status/READINESS.adoc`), this is an abstract,
  provisional position paper.
* *Not a replacement for the interpretability literature.* It is a proposed vocabulary
  _over_ it — the same relationship the workbench bears to type theory.
* *Not neuro-symbolic hand-waving.* The symbolic side is the estate's *formal* calculus
  (`trope-checker`), not an LLM's paraphrase of one. The estate's own doctrine is to
  distrust the neural for exactness and keep invariants in the formal layer.
