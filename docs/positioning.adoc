= Where this sits among the formal disciplines
:toc: macro

The README's "What this is not" deliberately declines rivalry claims, and that
stands. This document *complements* it — it does not replace or soften it. Where
that section says what the vocabulary is not competing with, this one says,
positively, where it sits beside four established formal topics, and what each of
them does that this does not (and vice versa).

toc::[]

== The four-quadrant map

Four mature disciplines each own one question about a thing moving through a
system. None of them owns this project's question.

=== Type theory — what a thing IS

Type theory classifies. A type says what kind of thing a term is, and the
discipline's guarantees flow from that classification. It can encode a great deal
(the README already concedes this); what it does not supply, by itself, is a
diagnostic vocabulary for the *individuating content of one particular instance*
and its fate under transformation.

=== Graded and coeffect type systems — how context USES a thing

Graded monads (Katsumata, POPL 2014), coeffect calculi (Petricek, Orchard,
Mycroft, ICFP 2014), Granule (Orchard, Liepelt, Eades, ICFP 2019), Quantitative
Type Theory (Atkey, LICS 2018; McBride, "I Got Plenty o' Nuttin'", 2016), and
Fuzz's sensitivity types (Reed & Pierce, ICFP 2010) annotate *how a context uses
a value*: how many times, at what sensitivity, under what resource discipline.

The family resemblance to this vocabulary is real — the sibling calculus in
`trope-checker` uses quantitative types for its loss-plumbing. The difference is
the *direction of the grade*. Their grades are *exogenous*: usage annotations
imposed by the context on an interchangeable value. The trope vocabulary's grades
are *endogenous*: they record how much of a particular's own individuating
content survived what was done to it. A Granule grade never says what the value
lost; a `p-residual` says nothing else.

=== Provenance — where a thing CAME FROM

Provenance semirings (Green, Karvounarakis, Tannen, PODS 2007), why- and
where-provenance (Buneman, Khanna, Tan, ICDT 2001), and the survey literature
(Cheney, Chiticariu, Tan, _Foundations and Trends in Databases_, 2009) record
derivation: which sources, combined how, produced this output.

Provenance is *truthful by construction* — the annotation is computed alongside
the query and cannot disagree with it. It therefore has no analogue of the
deceptive trio: nothing in a semiring polynomial can assert a false value
(`p-falsifying`), a false bearer (`p-misbinding`), or a false singleness
(`p-conflating`). Deception is outside its object language; here it is a third of
the effect vocabulary.

=== Information-flow control — who may SEE a thing

IFC (Denning, CACM 1976; Myers & Liskov's Decentralized Label Model; Sabelfeld &
Sands, "Declassification: Dimensions and Principles", JCS 2009) restricts
observation. Labels are *contamination on an observer lattice*: they propagate to
say who may now see a derived value. A label never describes what remains of the
value's individuating content — a fully redacted datum and a pristine one can
carry the same label. Declassification policies are the nearest thing to a
use-model, and the table below says where that nearness ends.

== The positioning sentence

None of the four says *how much of a particular's individuating content survives
a transformation, and whether the residue is licensed for a given use*. That
judgment — loss named per effect, residue inspected, verdict relative to a
declared use-model — is what this vocabulary, and the sibling calculus in
`github.com/hyperpolymath/trope-checker`, names and checks.

== Nearest neighbours, term by term

Twelve terms, each with its nearest concept in the four fields and one line on
where the neighbour falls short. These are informal correspondences for
orientation, not claimed equivalences.

[cols="1,2,3"]
|===
| Term | Nearest neighbour | Where the neighbour falls short

| `p-preserving`  | Identity lineage in provenance; grade `1` exact usage in QTT/Granule
| Records that nothing was consumed or derived — not that the instance's individuating content is intact.

| `p-projecting`  | Projection in relational lineage (where-provenance through `SELECT`)
| Lineage names which columns survived, but carries no judgment that the dropped ones were part of what individuated the instance.

| `p-collapsing`  | Predicate abstraction; a boolean-typed coercion
| Soundness is judged against the predicate, not against the particular — the abstraction is correct even though _which_ quack is gone.

| `p-detaching`   | Where-provenance losing its source cell
| Tracks the value's source location as bookkeeping; it does not treat the quality–bearer bond as constitutive, so its loss raises no flag.

| `p-attenuating` | Fuzz/DP function sensitivity (Reed & Pierce)
| Bounds numeric distance between outputs; says nothing about which unstructured part of the individuating content degraded.

| `p-falsifying`  | Integrity/taint labels in IFC
| Taint marks untrusted *origin*; it cannot express that a record asserts faithfulness while presenting a value that was never there.

| `p-misbinding`  | A bad join / foreign-key violation; why-provenance witnesses
| Provenance is truthful by construction — a wrong binding is a fault outside its model, not an expressible effect with a named dual.

| `p-conflating`  | Duplicate elimination under set semantics (multiplicity dropped from the semiring)
| The semiring *keeps* multiplicity precisely because losing it is destructive — but it has no veridicality axis to call the unlabelled merge deceptive.

| `p-fusing`      | Aggregation with lineage (group-by provenance)
| Records which inputs contributed to the aggregate, but issues no licence about what downstream may still do with the fused residue.

| `p-residual`    | The residual grade/capability left on a coeffect-typed variable
| That grade is an exogenous bound on further *use*; it does not describe what of the instance itself survived.

| `use-model`     | A declassification policy (Sabelfeld & Sands dimensions); a privacy budget
| Says who may see, or how much may leak — not what a declared *use* needs of a particular in order to reason correctly from it.

| verdict (`p-sufficient` / `p-insufficient`) | A policy or type check
| Judges conformance to an exogenous specification; the verdict here judges the *sufficiency of a residue* relative to the declared use, with a witness edge.
|===

== Open obligations, stated plainly

Consistent with `AFFIRMATION.adoc`, the honest edges:

* Whether the three loss-coordinates (fate, bond, merge) are exhaustive is open
  (calculus O1-coord) — there may be a fourth way to individuate-and-lose.
* The table above is orientation, not theorem: no adequacy or embedding results
  relate the vocabulary's effects to graded modalities, semiring annotations, or
  IFC lattices. If such results are ever claimed, they belong in the calculus
  repository with proofs, not here.
* The endogenous/exogenous contrast is a design claim about what the grades
  *record*, not a claim that graded systems could not be re-purposed to encode
  it. The README's second boundary ("not 'type theory cannot express this'")
  applies to this document too.
