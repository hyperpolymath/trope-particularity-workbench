= Baffled Couch Potatoes: On the Neglect of Trope Theory
:revnumber: 0.1 (draft for review)
:revdate: 2026-07-03
:toc: macro

[abstract]
An outreach essay. I asked a large language model about trope theory and it wanted to
talk about Coronation Street. When it finally found the philosophers' sense, it decided
there was "basically nothing going on there." Both reactions are symptoms of the same
neglect — and the neglect is a solvable problem, not a verdict on the subject.

toc::[]

== A conversation with a couch potato

I went to a well-known AI assistant to think aloud about *trope theory*. It was, at
first, charmingly baffled. It assumed I meant tropes in the television sense — the
plot furniture of _Coronation Street_ and _Doctor Who_ — and kept trying to steer me
back to the sofa. When I insisted, and it went and read the Stanford Encyclopedia entry,
its considered view was that trope theory is a quiet backwater where, to paraphrase,
basically nothing is going on.

This is a machine trained on a very large fraction of everything humans have written.
Its instinct was that "trope" means telly, and its second instinct — once corrected —
was that the philosophical version is inert. Neither instinct is stupid. Both are
_evidence_: about what has been written, and about what has not.

== Two neglects, one word

The word "trope" has been culturally captured. In common usage it means a recurring
motif in fiction; there are wikis with hundreds of thousands of pages lovingly
cataloguing the television kind. The philosophers' *trope* — a property considered as a
particular, non-repeatable instance, _this_ redness here rather than the universal
"redness" — was named by D. C. Williams in 1953, who called tropes "the very alphabet
of being", and developed by Keith Campbell in 1990. It is a serious, live position in
metaphysics, with a Stanford Encyclopedia entry and a steady trickle of journal work.

But it is a _trickle_. And here is the deeper neglect, the one that matters for this
project: classic trope theory built a magnificent *statics* and almost no *dynamics*. It
told us, with great care, what a property-instance _is_. It said almost nothing about
what _happens_ to one as it moves through a system — when it is flattened to a checkbox,
cut loose from the thing it was a quality of, quietly degraded, or — worse — replaced by
a false value while still claiming to be faithful. The metaphysics of abstract
particulars has a rich account of being and a threadbare account of _loss_.

== Why this leaves a machine helpless

A language model has no vocabulary it did not read. If almost nobody wrote down the
dynamics of property-instance loss, the model has no words for it — and so, confronted
with a question that is _entirely about_ that dynamics, it does the only thing it can:
it reaches for the nearest well-populated neighbour and starts talking about soap opera.

This is not a failure of intelligence. It is a failure of _supply_. The model is a
mirror; it reflects a gap in the written record. When we mock it for confusing
metaphysics with _Doctor Who_, we are really pointing at ourselves — at a discipline
that left one of its most useful ideas half-built.

And the idea is useful in exactly the places modern computing is currently struggling.
Every system that summarises, embeds, compresses, or paraphrases is doing something to a
property-instance: keeping some of _this-quality-here_ and discarding the rest, and
usually with no honest record of which. A neural network's "features" are, arguably,
tropes — particular instances, not universals — and its worst-understood pathologies
(features silently collapsing into one another; a probe attributing a property the model
never had) are, arguably, the very *loss and deception* moves trope theory forgot to
theorise. (That argument is made properly, and carefully hedged, in the companion piece
`../../theory/computing/trope-theory-and-neural-representation.adoc`.)

== The repair is already under way

The point of this essay is not to lament. It is to say that the missing layer is
_buildable_, and that building it is what this estate is for.

* A *vocabulary* — nine named effects for what can happen to a property-instance, split
  by how much is lost and by whether what survives is _true_
  (`trope-particularity-workbench`).
* A *calculus and a checker* — a formal, machine-verified core that composes those
  effects and returns a verdict, relative to a declared use, with a witness when the
  answer is "not enough" (`trope-checker`; alpha, its conformance suite green, its
  grade-algebra boundary proved in Lean, its metatheory under way in Agda).

None of this is finished, and this essay claims nothing about it that the repositories'
own honesty snapshots do not. The statics we inherited from Williams and Campbell; the
dynamics is the work. Three of trope theory's four classic problems — swapping, piling,
and the resemblance-regress — still have no formal image even here. That is not
embarrassing; it is the map of what to do next
(`../../status/RESEARCH-PROGRAMME.adoc`).

== The moral

Neglect is self-reinforcing. Because the dynamics went unwritten, the machines cannot
speak it; because the machines cannot speak it, it looks like there is nothing to say;
because it looks like there is nothing to say, it stays unwritten. The way out of that
loop is not to scold the couch potato. It is to write the missing words down —
precisely enough that a machine could one day check them — and then to fund the writing.

The tragedy of trope theory's neglect is that it left us articulate about _being_ and
speechless about _loss_, at exactly the moment we built machines whose entire job is to
lose things gracefully. Fixing that is a small field's chance to matter a great deal.
