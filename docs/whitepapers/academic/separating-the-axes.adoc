= Separating the Axes: A Unified Dissolution of the Classic Objections to Trope Theory
:revnumber: 0.1 (working paper — draft for review; synthesises the WP-1 notes)
:revdate: 2026-07-17
:toc: macro
:sectnums:
:sectnumlevels: 3

[abstract]
The four classic objections to trope theory — the problem of *individuation*, the
*swapping* (transposition) objection, the *piling* (stacking) objection, and Russell's
*resemblance-regress* — have been treated, for seventy years, as four separate problems
with four separate replies. This paper argues they are one problem in four disguises.
Each trades on *conflating a standing ontological fact about tropes with an operational,
evidential, or use-relative fact that a provenance-enriched, operation-graded framework
tracks on a distinct axis*; and each dissolves the same way — by *separating an axis*,
never by *adding an entity*. The framework that makes the separations available is the
hyperpolymath trope stack, whose four-field trope (quality, bearer, context, record) and
graded calculus of operations were built to account for *loss*, not to answer these
objections — which is what makes their uniform surrender evidence rather than
gerrymandering. The paper is honest about three things: the dissolutions have *uneven*
formal backing (one rests on a machine-checked theorem, one on partly-proved structure,
one on philosophical relocation only); they defend a *conservative extension* of
classical trope theory, not bare Williams; and the exhaustiveness claim they suggest
(that four axes are *all* the axes) is a conjecture, not a result. This is a working
paper synthesising three provisional research notes, offered for scrutiny.

== The situation

Trope theory — the doctrine that the world's properties are *abstract particulars*,
non-repeatable instances (`this` redness here, not the universal shared across bearers)
— was named by D. C. Williams (1953) and developed by Keith Campbell (1990). It has
always been sold on ontological economy and common sense, and it has always been
answered by a standard battery of objections:

* *Individuation.* What makes `this` trope the very one it is? Bare haecceity looks
  mysterious; qualitative individuation collapses exactly-alike tropes into one.
* *Swapping* (transposition). Permute two exactly-resembling tropes between their
  bearers: nothing is detectable, yet if the tropes are two, the worlds differ. Either
  a difference makes no difference, or the tropes' particularity does no work.
* *Piling* (stacking). What bars many exactly-alike tropes from co-piling on one bearer?
  Permit it and the ontology is profligate; bar it and the bar looks universal-shaped.
* *Resemblance-regress* (Russell 1911, 1912). Shared kind is explained by resemblance
  between tropes; but resemblance is either a universal (nominalism abandoned) or a
  further trope whose being-a-resemblance regenerates the problem one level up.

The literature treats these as a *checklist*: a trope theorist is expected to have a
reply to each, and the replies are independent (internal relations for the regress,
primitive particularity for individuation, and so on). This paper's claim is that the
checklist is an artefact. The four objections are four symptoms of a single lack, and a
single structural feature dispatches all four at once.

== The lack, and what supplies it

The lack is this: *bare* trope theory has only tropes. A bare trope is a quality borne
by a bearer, and that is the whole ontology. So when an objection turns on a distinction
between *what a trope is* and *what happens to it*, or between *how many tropes there are*
and *how many a use needs*, or between *a relation* and *a claim about that relation*,
bare trope theory has no vocabulary to draw the distinction — and collapses the two. The
objections are precisely the places where that collapse bites.

The framework used here supplies the missing vocabulary along two dimensions, neither
introduced to answer these objections.

*Provenance-enrichment.* The trope is enriched from two fields to four: the situated
`quality`, the `bearer`, the `context` that individuates it, and an honest `record` of
how the record came to be (the veridicality axis). The enrichment is a deliberate
departure from Williams, recorded as such, and — critically — it is a *conservative
extension*: by a four-part compatibility test (it contradicts nothing the base asserts;
connects by writable bridges; requires no change to the base; does non-redundant work on
a domain the base is silent about) the enriched trope *contains* the classical one, which
is recovered by projecting `context` and `record` away. §7 returns to whether this
"changes the subject."

*Operation-grading.* Operations *on* tropes carry a *grade* — a record of what the
operation did, in three orthogonal coordinates: `fate` (per-field survival), `bond`
(the quality–bearer tie), and `merge` (instance cardinality). Grades compose by a fixed
algebra; on the ratified carrier (R-2026-07-07) that algebra is a *commutative* monoid,
machine-checked in Lean 4. A declared *use-model* then yields a decidable *verdict*:
whether what survived is *enough for the use at hand*. The decisive point is that a grade
describes a *verb's residue*, not a *noun's essence*: grades are about operations, not
about the standing identity of tropes.

Four fields and three grade-coordinates give the framework *seven axes* on which a fact
can live, where bare trope theory had one (the trope). The objections, we now show, each
conflate two of them.

== The four dissolutions

Each dissolution has the same three-part shape: the *conflation* the objection trades on;
the *axis-separation* that undoes it; and a *discriminating test* that distinguishes the
dissolution from a mere refusal to take the objection seriously. Fuller treatments of the
last three are in the companion notes (`docs/theory/ontologies/`).

=== Individuation — quality vs context

*Conflation.* The dilemma (mysterious haecceity vs collapsing qualitative individuation)
assumes individuation must be carried *either* by a bare thisness *or* by the quality
exemplified. Bare trope theory, having only quality-and-bearer, has nowhere else to put
it.

*Separation.* Individuation lives on the `context` axis — the situating conditions,
`this`-here-now — which is *distinct* from the `quality` axis (what is exemplified). Two
exactly-alike tropes share a quality and differ in context; they are two *because* their
contexts differ, without a bare haecceity and without letting the shared quality collapse
them. Individuation is *contextual*, neither primitive-mysterious nor qualitative.

*Test.* The account predicts a graded notion — "recoverable individuating content" — on
which an operation can degrade *how individuated* a trope remains without touching *what*
individuates it; a primitivist has only the all-or-nothing "same or not."

=== Swapping — numerical identity vs process-grade

*Conflation.* The dilemma assumes that if two exactly-alike transposed tropes are really
two, their distinctness must show up as a *detectable* difference in the world's workings.

*Separation.* Numerical distinctness is a standing fact about the tropes (the identity
axis); what a transposition *does* is a grade. Because grade composition is commutative
(a *machine-checked* theorem on the ratified carrier), permuting interchangeable inputs
is a grade-level identity: nothing detectable-through-process changes. Yet the tropes stay
two — numerical distinctness was never a grade. The swap is *grade-invariant without being
identity-collapsing*; the "difference that makes no difference" is a numerical difference
(real) coinciding with a dynamical one (nil, provably).

*Test.* Swapping-invariance is exactly co-extensive with exact resemblance: introduce any
inexactness and the swap leaves a graded trace. Neither the verificationist (swaps always
meaningless) nor the bundle theorist (no distinctness to preserve) predicts that boundary.
This is the *strongest* of the four dissolutions: it rests on a proved law.

=== Piling — ontological count vs use-relative count

*Conflation.* The dilemma (profligate if permitted, universal-shaped if barred) assumes
the number of co-located exactly-alike tropes is a brute fact the theory must settle.

*Separation.* Cardinality lives on the `merge` coordinate (`Single ⊐ Fused(τ) ⊐
Conflated`, ordered by recoverable plurality); sufficiency of a count for a purpose lives
on the verdict. The theory does not *settle* the count; it *records* it and *judges* it.
Honest piling is `Fused(τ)` — labelled, recoverable — so never *silently* profligate;
silent piling is `Conflated` — grade-detectable, failing every honest floor. And the only
constraint on piling is that a merge be *labelled*: a constraint on the *record*, not the
tropes. By the compatibility test, that touches the ontology nowhere — so the "a bar
smuggles in universals" horn fails, because *there is no ontological bar*.

*Test.* The account permits labelled piling and faults only silent piling — a boundary
the flat "no two alike on one bearer" cannot draw, and one universals cannot even *state*
(a universal cannot be had twice, so it "avoids the piling question only by being unable
to ask it"). Formal backing: the `merge` order is specified and the `Conflated`-as-bottom
result is machine-checked; the reading of piling as a merge situation is philosophy.

=== Resemblance-regress — the relation vs the claim

*Conflation.* Russell's regress asks "what makes this resemblance a resemblance?" and
treats the answer as needing a *further* resemblance. The question equivocates between
the resemblance *relation* and a *claim* that the relation holds.

*Separation.* The relation is *internal* — grounded wholly in the natures of its terms
(the classical response, adopted here, not improved) — so it does not iterate: there is
no further entity to ground. The *claim* lives on the `record` axis; its warrant is an
attestation, and that attestation's warrant a further one, down an *evidential* chain
that terminates at the *O5 world-access boundary* (where a claim is finally checked
against the world, not another claim). An evidential regress that bottoms out in
world-access is not vicious. The regress-generating step arises only if one conflates the
self-grounding relation with the warrant-needing claim.

*Test.* Ask where the regress stops: the account names a boundary (O5 world-access);
"resemblance grounds resemblance" answers "nowhere," which is the vice. And a *false*
resemblance-claim becomes a locatable `Conflated`-style record fault — a distinction
bare-trope nominalism cannot state. This is the *weakest* of the four: nothing here rests
on a theorem, it depends on the O5 proposal being adopted, and it defers the ontological
regress entirely to internal relations.

== The unifying diagnosis

Set the four side by side and the pattern is unmistakable. Each objection conflates a
*standing ontological fact about tropes* with something the enriched-and-graded framework
tracks *elsewhere*:

[cols="1,2,2,1",options="header"]
|===
| Objection | Ontological fact | Conflated with | Home axis
| Individuation | which trope this is | what quality it bears | `context`
| Swapping | that the tropes are two | what an operation does | grade (commutativity) / `bond`
| Piling | how many tropes there are | how many a use needs | `merge` / verdict
| Regress | that two tropes resemble | the claim that they do | `record`
|===

Bare trope theory ran each pair together because it had *only* the left column — the
tropes — and no axis for operations, cardinality-records, uses, or evidential claims.
The four objections are therefore not four independent problems but *four symptoms of one
lack*: the absence, in bare trope theory, of axes distinct from the tropes themselves.
Supply the axes — `context`, the grade coordinates, the `record` — and grade the
*operations* rather than the tropes, and the objections dissolve *uniformly*: each by
separating an axis, none by adding an entity. That last clause matters. Russell's own fix
*added an entity* (the universal resemblance); haecceitism *adds* a primitive thisness.
Axis-separation adds nothing to the ontology; it adds *structure around* it — structure
that (by the compatibility test) the ontology was silent about and is not perturbed by.

== The exhaustiveness conjecture

That the four canonical objections distribute *one-to-one* across the framework's four
trope-level axes (`quality`-vs-`context`, the trope-vs-grade divide, `merge`, `record`) is
striking, and it suggests a conjecture the framework already names as its central open
question (O1-coord): *are these axes the exhaustive ways to individuate-and-lose?* The
distribution is *defeasible evidence* for exhaustiveness — if the axes carved arbitrarily,
one would not expect the historically-independent objections to fall so cleanly, one to
each. It is not a proof. What would bear on it:

* *Confirming.* A *fifth* classic objection that also dissolves by separating one of the
  same four axes strengthens the conjecture.
* *Disconfirming.* An objection that resists all four coordinates — that cannot be read as
  a conflation of any ontological fact with any of `context` / grade / `merge` / `record`
  — is evidence for a *fifth* axis, and would falsify the exhaustiveness claim as stated.

This reframes a notoriously hard metaphysical question (how many kinds of individuation-
and-loss are there?) as one with a foothold: the *distribution of the known objections*.
That foothold is the paper's methodological contribution, distinct from the four
dissolutions themselves.

== Objections and replies

*"You have changed the subject."* The dissolutions use `context`, `record`, and
operation-grades — machinery classical (bare) trope theory lacks. A hardcore Williamsian
who refuses the enrichment does not get the dissolutions. *Reply, conceded in part:* the
paper defends a *conservative extension* of classical trope theory, and says so. The
extension is conservative in a precise sense (the compatibility test): it contradicts,
changes, and removes nothing in the base; it only adds axes the base was silent about.
The enriched trope *contains* the classical trope. One is therefore not defending a rival
theory but *equipping* trope theory with the distinctions its objections always demanded
— distinctions a theory is entitled to draw. Still, the honest scope is: *enriched* trope
theory, not bare Williams.

*"The dissolutions are unevenly rigorous."* True, and flagged throughout: swapping rests
on a machine-checked commutativity theorem; piling on specified structure with a proved
bottom but a philosophical reading; individuation and the regress on philosophical
relocation alone (the regress additionally depending on the still-unratified O5 proposal).
The paper claims *uniformity of the move*, not uniformity of proof. Presenting a graded
confidence is itself in keeping with the framework's no-overclaim discipline.

*"Axis-separation is gerrymandered to fit."* The strongest worry: if the axes were chosen
to dissolve these objections, their fit proves nothing. *Reply:* the axes were *not* so
chosen. The four-field trope and the three grade-coordinates were built to account for
*loss and deception of property-instances* in computational pipelines — a use-case with no
stake in the metaphysics-of-properties debate. That an independently-motivated
loss-accounting apparatus turns out to separate exactly the axes the classic objections
conflate is the evidential force of the paper. The falsifiability of the exhaustiveness
conjecture (§6) is the check against post-hoc fit.

*"Internal relations do the real work in the regress."* Granted — and stated. For the
regress the estate contributes *relocation and disambiguation*, not a new ontological
ground; the ontological regress stands or falls with the internal-relations reply
independently of anything here.

== Conclusion

The classic objections to trope theory are one objection wearing four masks. Each conflates
a standing fact about tropes with an operational, cardinal, or evidential fact; each
dissolves by separating an axis; none is answered by adding an entity. The framework that
makes the separations available was built for loss-accounting, not for this debate, and one
of its four dissolutions is machine-checked — which is why the uniform result reads as a
discovery about the objections rather than a tailoring of the theory. The same framework
turns the exhaustiveness question (are four axes all the axes?) into a conjecture with a
falsification condition. What remains is real: the per-problem checker fixtures the notes
still owe, the ratification of O5, and — above all — the adjudication of exhaustiveness
(O1-coord). But the shape is now visible: *defend the axes, and the tropes defend
themselves.*
