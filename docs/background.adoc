= Background — trope theory and the compatibility test
:toc: macro
:sectnums:

The vocabulary's centre — qualities as borne by _this_ thing, in context — is the
founding move of *trope theory*, the metaphysics of abstract particulars. This
document gives the grounding and the test a candidate term must pass to belong.

toc::[]

== Tropes as abstract particulars (Williams, Campbell)

A *trope* is a property considered as a *non-repeatable, situated instance* — _this_
redness, _this_ hoarseness, here, borne by _this_ thing — not the universal "redness"
shared across many bearers. The position was named and defended by D. C. Williams
("On the Elements of Being", 1953) and developed by Keith Campbell (_Abstract
Particulars_, 1990). Two tropes can be *exactly alike* and still be *two*: exact
resemblance is not numerical identity. Keeping those apart is the distinction the
whole theory is built on — and the distinction `p-conflating` violates.

The mapping this workbench borrows:

* a *property-instance* is a _trope_;
* *whose it is* (the bearer) is _compresence_ — on the bundle view, an object just
  _is_ a bundle of mutually compresent tropes;
* `p-conflating` is the collapse of _exact resemblance_ into _numerical identity_:
  asserting two exactly-alike tropes are one. `p-fusing` is its honest twin — the
  same two-into-one move, but labelled, so the plurality stays recoverable.

== A deliberate departure: provenance-enrichment

Classical tropes are _quality borne by a bearer_. This workbench (and the calculus
it feeds) works on a *provenance-enriched* trope — a property-instance carrying four
fields: the situated *quality*, the *bearer*, the *context* that individuates it, and
an honest *record* of how it came to be. The enrichment is a departure from Williams,
recorded explicitly so downstream readers do not attribute it to him. It is what lets
the vocabulary speak of `record`-level honesty — the veridicality axis on which the
three deceptive duals live.

== What trope theory does not supply

Trope theory grounds the _statics_ — what a property-instance is. It does not supply
the *dynamics of loss and deception*: collapsing a quality to a checkbox, attenuating
its fidelity, detaching it from its bearer, falsifying its value, mis-binding it to a
false bearer, and the *sufficiency verdict* that judges the residue against a declared
use. That layer is this project's own.

== The compatibility test

A candidate effect is a *compatible* addition to the ontology — not merely a
_consistent_ one — only if all four hold:

. it *contradicts nothing* the base asserts;
. its connection to the base is a *writable bridge* (a named relation with stated
  conditions), not a quiet redefinition of the base's terms;
. it requires *no change to the base* (a conservative extension); and
. it does *non-redundant work* on a domain the base does not cover.

*Worked: the deceptive trio passes.* `p-conflating`, `p-falsifying`, and
`p-misbinding` are claims about *records* (what is asserted, and whether honestly),
not about tropes. The bridges are:

* `p-conflating` — "a singular record is faithful only if its referent is in fact one
  trope";
* `p-falsifying` — "a record's value is faithful only if it is the value the trope in
  fact had";
* `p-misbinding` — "a record's bearer is faithful only if it is the trope's actual
  bearer".

Each bridge touches the ontology *nowhere* (it constrains records, not tropes), needs
*no change* to the base, and does *new work* the ontology is silent on
(record-veridicality). The faithful effects (`p-projecting`, `p-collapsing`,
`p-detaching`, `p-attenuating`, `p-fusing`) earn their places by the same test, on the
loss-amount axis rather than the veridicality one.

== Note on the open metaphysical question

Whether the three loss-coordinates (fate, bond, merge) are themselves *exhaustive* —
that there is no fourth way to individuate-and-lose — is a philosophy-of-individuation
question (calculus O1-coord), left open and logged, not settled here.
