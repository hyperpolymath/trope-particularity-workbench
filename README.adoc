= Trope Particularity
:toc:
:toc-placement: preamble

image:https://img.shields.io/badge/License-MPL_2.0-blue.svg[License: MPL-2.0,link="https://opensource.org/licenses/MPL-2.0"]
image:https://img.shields.io/badge/status-specification-orange[Status: specification]

A diagnostic vocabulary for *property-instance individuation and loss*. It names —
precisely, with a fixed and deliberately small set of terms — the ways a specific,
situated quality gets flattened as it moves through a system, and whether what
survives is *enough for the use at hand*.

[quote]
____
Duck typing asks whether it quacks. Particularity asks which quack you just
laundered into a boolean.
____

== What this is

A *workbench*, not a language. The artefact is a vocabulary you think _with_: a
board of named tools you pick up to say what just happened to a property-instance.

A *property-instance* is a quality as borne by _this_ entity, in _this_ context,
under _these_ conditions — the hoarseness of _this_ duck, this morning, on this
pond. Not the universal "hoarseness"; the particular, situated instance of it.

The vocabulary sorts into *three kinds of term*, because they are three different
kinds of thing:

* *Effects* — things that _happen_ to a property-instance (nine terms).
* *Residue* — what is _left_ after an effect (one term).
* *Verdict* — a _judgement_ on whether the residue was enough, always relative to a
  declared *use-model* (three terms: the use-model and the two verdicts).

.The twelve terms, by family
image::docs/img/word-families.svg[A property-instance passes through one of nine effects, leaving a residual, judged against a use-model as sufficient or insufficient,width=680]

See also `assets/trope-particularity-map.svg` (the pipeline, with the effects split
two ways: by *loss amount* and by *veridicality*) and `assets/particularity-trainer.html`
(an interactive drill on naming the nine effects).

The shape of the whole thing in one line:

____
A property-instance undergoes some *p-effect*; relative to its declared
*use-model*, the surviving *residual* is judged *p-sufficient* or *p-insufficient*.
____

== What this is not

Three boundaries keep the project honest:

. *Not "trope safety."* This is not a rival to type safety sold on the same ground.
  That comparison is a losing one, and not the point.
. *Not "type theory cannot express this."* Type theory can encode many such
  structures, and so can trope theory. The claim is narrower: neither, _by itself_,
  supplies the diagnostic vocabulary for property-instance individuation and loss.
. *Not a bag of subtle metadata.* The centre stays fixed: individuated
  property-instances. A term that does not bear on individuation or loss does not
  belong here.

And a practical boundary for when any of this matters at all:

____
Particularity matters when loss, detachment, conflation, deception, or portability
of property-instances affects interpretation, correctness, accountability, or later
use.
____

== The vocabulary

=== Effects — what happens to a property-instance

The nine effects split two independent ways. *Loss amount*: kept whole
(`p-preserving`) versus lost-or-changed. *Veridicality*: faithful versus *deceptive*
— the three deceptive duals (`p-falsifying`, `p-misbinding`, `p-conflating`) present
something false rather than merely lossy.

[cols="1,1,1,3"]
|===
| Term | Arity | Veridicality | What it is

| `p-preserving`  | one | faithful   | The baseline: nothing lost. Quality, bearer, context, and an honest record all survive. Every other effect is _preserving minus one part_ (or a lie about one part).
| `p-projecting`  | one | faithful   | Named parts dropped *on purpose*. You know exactly which coordinates are gone. A reduced view, not a lie — _provided nobody mistakes the projection for the whole_.
| `p-collapsing`  | one | faithful   | Flattened to a predicate or scalar. The quality survives as a _test_; the individual that bore it is gone. The quack becomes `true`.
| `p-detaching`   | one | faithful   | The quality severed from its bearer — the quality–bearer bond broken. A trait floating free of whose it was.
| `p-attenuating` | one | faithful   | Fidelity degraded with no clean structure. Unlike projecting, you have lost an _unknown_ amount.
| `p-falsifying`  | one | *deceptive* | A *false value*: the residual claims faithfulness, but the quality presented is not the one that was there. Not loss — substitution.
| `p-misbinding`  | one | *deceptive* | A *false bearer*: the bond is asserted intact, but the quality is pinned to the wrong entity. The tie holds — to the wrong one.
| `p-conflating`  | two | *deceptive* | A *false singleness*: two numerically distinct instances asserted to be *the same one*, with no label saying so. Downstream reasons about one where there were two, and is silently wrong.
| `p-fusing`      | two | faithful   | Two instances deliberately blended into one *labelled* aggregate. Not an error — the plurality survives in the label, so downstream knows not to pull them apart.
|===

The arity column marks a real seam: *seven single-instance* effects, and *two
two-instance* (`p-conflating`, `p-fusing` — the dishonest and honest versions of the
same two-into-one move). The veridicality column marks the other seam: the three
deceptive duals are *false value / false bearer / false singleness*. The two splits
are independent — loss-amount is about _how much_ survived; veridicality is about
_whether what survived is true_.

[NOTE]
====
The three deceptive duals exist in the vocabulary because the formal semantics needs
them, but none is *writable* in a faithful producer: a value-substitution, a
mis-attribution, or an untagged merge is a _fault_, caught at the checker's
validation boundary, not a move you compose with (see the checker's `prevent`
profile, R-2026-06-22).
====

=== Residue — what is left

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Term | What it is
| `p-residual` | What actually survived an effect. The handle you inspect to decide whether the loss mattered.
|===

=== Verdict — relative to a declared use-model

A verdict is *meaningless on its own*. The same collapse is sufficient for counting
ducks and insufficient for diagnosing one — nothing about the collapse changed, only
the use. So a verdict always names the model it is relative to.

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Term | What it is
| `use-model`      | The declared statement of what a property-instance is _for_. The thing every verdict is relative to.
| `p-sufficient`   | The surviving residual is *enough* for the declared use. What was lost did not matter _for this purpose_.
| `p-insufficient` | The surviving residual is *not enough* for the declared use. Something needed was lost (or falsified), and reasoning from the residue will go wrong.
|===

== Background

The vocabulary's centre — qualities as borne by _this_ thing, in context — is the
founding move of *trope theory* (the metaphysics of abstract particulars; D. C.
Williams, Keith Campbell). The full grounding and the compatibility test are in
`docs/background.adoc`. In short: a *property-instance* is a _trope_; *whose it is*
is _compresence_; and `p-conflating` is exactly the collapse of _exact resemblance_
into _numerical identity_ — two tropes can be exactly alike and still be two.

What trope theory does _not_ supply is the dynamics of *loss and deception* —
collapsing, attenuating, detaching, falsifying, mis-binding, and the sufficiency
verdict. That layer is this project's own.

== Status

Early, but no longer alone. This repository is the *vocabulary* — the named effects,
the residual, the verdict, their discriminating tests, and worked examples.

The *checker is a separate repository*: `github.com/hyperpolymath/trope-checker`. It
is a _checker, not a language_ — it consumes a language-neutral *Trope IR* and
returns a verdict (`p-sufficient` / `p-insufficient`) against each consumer's
declared use-model, with a witness edge. Its reference core is in Idris2 (quantitative
types track the loss-plumbing). The first front-end that lowers to the IR is the
*Haec* language (`github.com/hyperpolymath/haec`). This workbench defines the words
both of them realise; it depends on neither.
